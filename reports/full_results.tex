\section{Summary}

This document lists all 57 MLflow runs from the evaluation: 32 runs that chose settings, and 25 runs on held-back questions (5 setups, each with 4 per-anchor child runs).

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
\item \textbf{Served setup (D):} hybrid search through Pinecone with \texttt{gemini-3.5-flash-lite}, with faithfulness 0.978 and 5\% refusals.
\item \textbf{Top score (C):} keyword-only retrieval with the same generator, with faithfulness 0.985 and 10\% refusals.
\item \textbf{Earlier project's setup (B):} faithfulness 0.928 and 18\% refusals, but higher relevancy when it answers (0.755 against 0.702 for D).
\item \textbf{Estimated evaluation spend:} \$0.47, because repeated settings reused saved answers.
\end{itemize}


\section{How to read the tables}

\textbf{Color key:} \swatch{chosen} chosen or served row (also bold) \quad \swatch{topscore} top score \quad \swatch{scoregood} faithfulness 0.97 or higher \quad \swatch{scoremid} 0.94 to 0.97 \quad \swatch{scorelow} below 0.94 \quad \swatch{refusalhigh} refusals 20\% or higher

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
\item \textbf{Faithful.:} share of an answer's claims supported by its retrieved passages. This is the only score used to choose settings.
\item \textbf{Relev.:} how closely questions generated from the answer match the question asked. \textbf{Rel.\ if ans.} leaves out refusals, which can score well by repeating the question's words.
\item \textbf{Right paper:} share of a question's source papers found among the retrieved passages.
\item \textbf{Refusals:} answers using a known refusal phrase, or with no factual claims. \textbf{Claims made:} answers with a faithfulness score. \textbf{Judge fails:} judge replies that could not be read.
\item \textbf{Retrieve, Generate, Total:} average seconds per question. \textbf{Cost:} estimated dollars for the run's questions, counted even when saved answers were reused.
\item \textbf{Winner rule:} highest faithfulness. Within 0.01, higher relevancy wins, then lower latency. A run cannot win if it refuses over 34\% of questions or makes claims on under 80\%.
\item \textbf{Questions:} each stage used 15 setting-selection questions on one anchor. The final test used 15 held-back questions per anchor, or 60 in total.
\end{itemize}

\section{Why setup D is served}

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
\item \textbf{It uses Pinecone:} keyword-only retrieval never queries Pinecone when answering, while D searches by keywords and by meaning, as the proposal describes.
\item \textbf{The test favours keywords:} question rules told the generator to name the method being asked about, so exact-word matching has an easy job. Real users paraphrase.
\item \textbf{It refuses less and is more consistent:} it refuses 5\% of questions against 10\% for C.
\item \textbf{The trade-offs:} D's relevancy when answered is 0.702 against 0.755 for B, and it takes 10.3 seconds against 3.6. Setup E is the faster alternative, at 3.9 seconds and 0.962 faithfulness.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limits of these results}

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
\item \textbf{Small samples:} each stage used 15 questions on one anchor, so small gaps may be noise, and a winner on one anchor may not win elsewhere.
\item \textbf{Stage winners do not simply add up:} changing only A's generator gave C a higher score than the combined winners.
\item \textbf{Questions were written by an LLM:} they test finding and using what is in the corpus, not what real researchers would ask. The team removed 13 that did not stand alone.
\item \textbf{Scores are LLM judgements:} they vary slightly between runs, and \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} judges its own answers in setups A, B and E.
\item \textbf{Faithfulness has blind spots:} an answer can be faithful to the wrong passages, so the right-paper rate is shown alongside it.
\item \textbf{Latency is not production latency:} it was measured on a laptop CPU, with free-tier models.
\item \textbf{The history was rebuilt:} MLflow runs were re-logged from saved answers after fixing measurement bugs, so MLflow's own timestamps and durations reflect that step, not the original model calls.
\item \textbf{Added setups:} D and E were tested after seeing A, B and C, so their comparison is slightly less clean than a fully planned one.
\end{itemize}
